Android phone or tablet

Google’s Family Link supervises the child’s Google account and the device itself.

DeviceAbout 18 minutesSource confidence: highChecked 10 September 2026

There is a version built for younger children

Google Kids Space, a home screen for children. A child home screen with Play, Read, Watch and Make tabs, drawing on teacher-approved apps, chosen books and YouTube Kids. It sits on top of a supervised Google account made through Family Link.

Worth knowing: It only comes pre-installed on certain tablets, among them Pixel Tablet, Lenovo, onn., Nokia, Medion and Multilaser. You cannot add it to any Android tablet, which is what catches most parents out.

The official page for Google Kids Space

What is already on it

  • YouTube, and it is the full version rather than YouTube Kids. The Kids app is a separate download
  • Chrome
  • Google Play
  • Google Messages

What to watch for

  • Apps sideloaded outside the Play Store bypass Play’s age ratings
  • The browser reaches everything app controls don’t
  • Overnight use

Before you start

  • You need to be 18+ with your own Google Account, and you and your child need to be in the same country.
  • Changes can take about five minutes to reach the device, so don’t panic if a setting doesn’t apply instantly.

Check these before you undo anything

Most of a bad setup can be fixed in place, so do not start by deleting accounts. These are the parts that cannot be taken back.

  • Correcting a Google birthday downward to under 13 starts a 14-day clock. Set up supervision or verify an adult age inside that window, or the account is disabled and then deleted 30 days later. Act inside the 14 days.
  • The minimum age is 16 rather than 13 in Ireland, Germany and Poland, so the same correction lands differently depending on where you are.
  1. 1

    Set up Family Link supervision

    It’s the foundation. Nothing else in this guide works without it.

    Family Link app, or familylink.google.com

    1. Install the Family Link app on your phone.
    2. Create a Google Account for your child if they’re under 13, or add supervision to their existing account.
    3. Follow the prompts on their device to link it. You’ll need the device in your hands.

    Family Link can supervise Android devices and Chromebooks. It cannot supervise iPhones or iPads.

    Who holds this: You set this from your own account, and your child cannot change it back.

    Already done this?

    If they already have an ordinary unsupervised Google account, you do not need to create a new one. Supervision can be added to the account they already have. Two conditions: the child has to agree, and they have to be physically present with their device. Once it is on, Google's rule is that children need parent approval to stop supervision until they turn 18, so a 13-year-old can no longer switch it off alone.

  2. 2

    Control app downloads and approvals

    The quickest way for controls to become irrelevant is a new app you didn’t know about.

    Family Link → [your child] → Controls → Apps

    1. Turn on approval for all Play Store downloads, including free apps.
    2. Set Play Store content ratings for apps, games, films and TV.
    3. Review what’s already installed and block anything that shouldn’t be there.
    Recommended by age
    Under 7Approval required for everything, apps rated PEGI 7 / ESRB E
    7–11Approval required for everything, apps rated PEGI 7 / ESRB E
    11–12Approval required for everything, apps rated PEGI 12 / ESRB E10+
    13–15Approval required for paid apps, ratings PEGI 12 / ESRB T
    16–17Notifications rather than approvals

    Google has the same hole and states it plainly: previous downloads, and content shared through Family Library, will not require your approval. So anything installed once can come back without a request reaching you. Set approval to All content rather than Paid content only, because the free apps are the ones that matter here.

    Who holds this: You set this from your own account, and your child cannot change it back.

  3. 3

    Control who can call and text them

    The closest Android has to the iPhone's communication limits, and the step most parents do not know exists. Worth being straight about what it is and is not: no parental control on Android or iPhone lets you read your child's messages. Google's does not offer it and neither does Apple's. What you can do is decide who is able to reach them in the first place, which is the part that actually prevents harm rather than discovering it afterwards.

    Family Link → [your child] → Controls → Contacts

    1. Open Family Link, choose your child, then Controls, then Contacts.
    2. Add the people they should be able to reach. You can add up to 100.
    3. Set who can call and text them: Anyone, Contacts I add and limited groups, or Only contacts I add.
    4. Leave requests on, so when they want to add a number it comes to you to approve.
    Recommended by age
    Under 7Only contacts I addOnly people you have approved can reach them. Nobody else can message, call or speak to them.
    7–11Only contacts I addOnly people you have approved can reach them. Nobody else can message, call or speak to them.
    11–12Only contacts I addOnly people you have approved can reach them. Nobody else can message, call or speak to them.
    13–15Contacts I add and limited groupsOnly people you have approved can reach them. Nobody else can message, call or speak to them.
    16–17Anyone, with a conversation about who gets throughAnyone on the platform can reach them.

    Calls and texts from anyone outside your list do not get through, and adding someone new needs your approval.

    The limit worth knowing before you rely on this. Google says contact limitations only apply to communications through certain apps including Google Messages, Samsung Messages, and Google Dialer. WhatsApp, Snapchat, Discord and the rest are untouched by it, so this locks the front door and leaves the side ones exactly as they were. Set those in their own guides.

    Who holds this: You set this from your own account, and your child cannot change it back.

  4. 4

    Filter web and search

    Chrome is the widest opening on the device.

    Family Link → [your child] → Controls → Content restrictions → Google Chrome / Google Search

    1. Set Chrome to Try to block explicit sites, or Only allow approved sites for younger children.
    2. Turn SafeSearch on and lock it.
    3. Add specific sites to allow or block as needed.
    Recommended by age
    Under 7Only allow approved sites
    7–11Only allow approved sites
    11–12Try to block explicit sites
    13–15Try to block explicit sites
    16–17Try to block explicit sites

    Who holds this: You set this from your own account, and your child cannot change it back.

  5. 5

    Set daily limits and bedtime

    Bedtime is the setting that changes the most in practice. Family Link locks the device rather than nagging, so agree how they reach you after it kicks in before the first night rather than during it.

    Family Link → [your child] → Controls → Daily limit / Bedtime

    1. Set daily limits, which can differ by day of the week.
    2. Set a bedtime schedule that locks the device.
    3. You can grant bonus time from your phone when it’s genuinely warranted.

    Who holds this: You set this from your own account, and your child cannot change it back.

  6. 6

    Decide on location sharing

    Useful for younger children. For teenagers, worth agreeing rather than imposing.

    Family Link → [your child] → Location

    1. Turn on location if you want it, and tell your child it’s on.
    2. For older teens, discuss it first. Covert tracking damages trust more than it protects.

    Who holds this: You set this from your own account, and your child cannot change it back.

  7. 7

    Set YouTube separately

    YouTube has its own content levels that sit inside Family Link.

    Family Link → [your child] → Controls → Content restrictions → YouTube

    1. Choose the content setting. Google uses two names for each: Older kids or Explore (9+), Teens or Explore more (13+), Older teens or Most of YouTube.
    2. Consider the YouTube Kids app instead for under-9s.

    Google's own pages disagree on these names and neither set carries a change notice, so you may see either. They are the same three settings.

    Who holds this: You set this from your own account, and your child cannot change it back.

Safety check

  • Family Link supervision active
  • Download approval on
  • Who can call and text them is set
  • Play content ratings set
  • SafeSearch on and locked
  • Chrome filtering set
  • Daily limit and bedtime set
  • YouTube content level set

Worth talking about

  • Show them the Family Link app so it isn’t a mystery.
  • Agree what happens when they want a new app, so it becomes a request rather than a fight.
  • Talk about sideloading if they’re technical enough to know the term.
